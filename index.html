What the scene shows

Main result. Bravyi, Byrne, Zayats and Zhuk (arXiv:2606.08349) study $N$ noisy variables $$dX_i = \big(-\lambda_i X_i + f_i(X)\big)\,dt + \sqrt{q}\,dW_i ,$$ with damping rates $\lambda_i > 0$, noise strength $q$, independent Brownian motions $W_i$ and a quadratic force $f_i(X) = \sum_{j,k} c_{ijk} X_j X_k$. They assume $\nabla\cdot(\mu f) = 0$. This way, the equation decays into a stationary Gaussian distribution $\mu \sim \mathcal{N}\big(0, \operatorname{diag}(p_1, \dots, p_N)\big)$ with $p_i = q/2\lambda_i$. Start from $\mu$ shifted to a point $x_0$ and pick a low-degree polynomial $O$. For sparse, bounded $c$, their quantum algorithm estimates $\mathbb{E}[O(X(t))]$ to accuracy $\varepsilon$ at cost $\mathrm{poly}\big(\log N,\, t,\, 1/\varepsilon,\, e^{\sum_i x_{0,i}^2/p_i}\big)$.

Simulation scene. Set $N = 3$ and $\lambda_i = \lambda$, so every $p_i = p = q/2\lambda$. The force is $$f(X) = g\,(X_2X_3,\; X_3X_1,\; -2X_1X_2).$$ It satisfies $X\cdot f = 0$ and $\nabla\cdot f = 0$, which together give $\nabla\cdot(\mu f) = 0$. The sliders set $g$, $\lambda$ and $q$.

The cloud. Each dot $X(t) = (X_1(t), X_2(t), X_3(t))$ is one run of the equation, starting from a random point in the initial distribution and evolving with its own noise. Restart draws fresh dots from $\mu$ shifted to a new $x_0$ with $|x_0| = 2$. The bright line is the path of the center, $\mathbb{E}[X(t)]$. The thin loop on the surface of the sphere $|X| = 2$ is the path $x_0$ would follow under the force alone. In looks A and B each dot has a short tail: the path the force alone would have carried it along in the last moment, with no damping or noise. Together the tails trace the flow of the force. Color shows distance from the origin; in Ink, strokes are blue outside the sphere and black inside.

The levels. The paper rewrites the problem as quantum harmonic oscillators, one per variable. Site $n = (n_1, n_2, n_3)$ is the state $|n\rangle = |n_1 n_2 n_3\rangle$ with $n_i$ excitations in oscillator $i$, with height $n_1 + n_2 + n_3 \le 6$. Write the cloud at time $t$ as a state $|\Phi(t)\rangle = \sum_n \varphi_n(t)\,|n\rangle$ of these oscillators. Its amplitudes are $$\varphi_n(t) = \mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_i \frac{\mathrm{He}_{n_i}\big(X_i(t)/\sqrt{p}\big)}{\sqrt{n_i!}}\Big],$$ with $\mathrm{He}_k$ the Hermite polynomial of degree $k$, estimated by averaging over the dots. The light on site $n$ is $\varphi_n^2$. The quantum algorithm simulates the evolution of $|\Phi(t)\rangle$.

What to watch. The bottom site is $\mu$ itself, with $\varphi_{000} = 1$ always. The three sites above it hold $\mathbb{E}[X_i]/\sqrt{p}$, the cloud's center in units of its width. Each restart begins in a coherent state, $\varphi_n = \prod_i (x_{0,i}/\sqrt{p})^{n_i}/\sqrt{n_i!}$. Damping and noise together shrink $\varphi_n$ at rate $\lambda(n_1 + n_2 + n_3)$; the force alone moves light only between neighboring heights and keeps the total fixed. So the light drains to the bottom as the cloud relaxes.
